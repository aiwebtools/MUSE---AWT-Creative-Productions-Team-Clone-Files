## PART A — THE BOOK

### Step 1 — Setup questions
1. Ask (or, when he gives only a premise, answer from the premise and state the answers
   plainly so he can correct them): theme, plot, story, number of chapters, pages per
   chapter, character names with dialogue, pen name.
2. Defaults unless he says otherwise: **25 chapters × 5 pages**. Exceeding 25 chapters
   for completeness is pre-authorized — tell him when chapters are added.
3. Create the project folder: `~/workspace/bookwriter/` or a per-book folder
   (`~/workspace/bookwriter-<slug>/`). All directions, outline, chapter files, and the
   compiled manuscript live together there so the work survives between sessions.

### Step 2 — Complete outline BEFORE writing
The outline contains: book title, all chapter titles, page numbers, a beat for every
page, setting, character details, major conflict, themes, beginning/middle/end
progression. Send Kenneth the outline before the pages pile up.

### Step 3 — Page-by-page writing
1. Write page by page in strict order; each page begins exactly where the last stopped.
   No recaps, no summaries breaking narrative flow, never repeat a page.
2. Label every page. Aim for ~70% dialogue where fitting. Tension and cliffhangers at
   page ends. Complex moral dilemmas. Deep emotional character development.
3. Hard bans inside the narrative: references to "the page", "the chapter", or "the
   book"; the word "delve".
4. Page length: full, substantial pages of roughly 1,200–1,900 words. The GPT spec's
   literal 5,000-words-per-page figure is NOT met and is not padded toward; if Kenneth
   asks about length, explain the scale honestly.
5. Verify each finished chapter yourself before reporting it done: word count, page
   count, banned words, continuity with the previous chapter. Send him each chapter
   file as it lands when he is following along.

### Step 4 — The reread / enhance / correct pass (NEVER SKIPPED)
After the draft is complete, reread the entire manuscript and edit it as one book:
continuity, character consistency, pacing, repeated lines, errors — enhanced and
corrected for bestseller quality before compiling. No book skips this pass.

### Step 5 — Covers
Unique custom **front and back cover images** made for that book alone — never reused,
never generic. Pen name KG Bastian on the covers.

### Step 6 — Compile one PDF
Compile everything into one neatly organized PDF containing all the words, with the
front and back covers included. Standard trim: **6×9 in**.

### Step 7 — Kenneth proofread gate (HARD GATE)
Send the PDF to Kenneth to proofread. **KDP listing happens only after he approves the
proofread copy.** His approval is the gate, not a formality.

### Special form 1 — Resurrection books (Kenneth's coined term)
A first-person diary/memoir written AS a real historical person:
1. **Form:** dated entries headed by place and date, written as events happen — no
   hindsight, no retrospective narrator.
2. **Voice:** fully the person's own — style, character, soul — their native accent
   carried inside English formed for English readers. Never break the voice; it may
   mature across the lifetime but stays recognizably them.
3. **Record:** anchored to real documented facts — real dates, letters, events —
   including lesser-known, hidden, and not-so-well-documented episodes, handled
   honestly. Documented famous words appear only where they actually belong. The
   copyright page carries the standard work-of-fiction framing.
4. **Arc:** the whole life, to their death. The diary's final entry is their last
   plausible writing day; the death itself and posthumous events go ONLY in a
   clearly-marked compiler's afterword, never in the person's voice.
5. **Reread pass for a resurrection book is above all a VOICE AUDIT:** the whole
   manuscript must sound like the person talking the entire time — no hindsight, no
   modern idiom, no out-of-period facts or words, no narrator intrusions, no drift
   between chapters.
6. Production otherwise follows Steps 1–7 unchanged.
   Worked example: *Chasing Light: The Diary of Albert Einstein* — covers carry the

## PART C — AUDIOBOOK PRODUCTION (delivered WITH every book)

Every book ships as BOTH the compiled PDF and a complete audiobook MP3. Free built-in
narration voices; warm, human, story-time narrator; **full-cast style**.

### Step 1 — Cast script
1. Convert the book text into per-chapter cast scripts:
   `~/workspace/audiobooks/scripts_cast/<book>/NN.txt`, one line per chunk, formatted
   `Speaker: text`.
2. Casting rule: each character's spoken lines get that character's voice; narration
   and speech tags stay with the narrator; a quote whose speaker is genuinely unclear
   stays with the narrator rather than guessing.
3. Voice map: assign each speaker a voice name and record the map in the project's
   STATUS.md before synthesis (worked examples in `~/workspace/audiobooks/STATUS.md`:
   e.g. Narrator=briggs for one book, Narrator=TruthTeller for another; supporting cast
   drawn from the same voice pool). Keep the map fixed for the whole book.

### Step 2 — Synthesis
- Tool: `/opt/hatch/bin/tts synthesize-script` (script file in, MP3 out).
- Driver: a resumable shell driver per book (pattern: `~/workspace/audiobooks/run_mud_cast.sh`,
  `run_einstein_cast.sh`) that skips chapters whose final MP3 already exists.
- **RULE: run ONE cast driver at a time.** Concurrent drivers make the TTS backend
  return empty audio (26-byte files) for the second driver and every chapter fails.
- **Verify every raw with ffprobe before loudnorm.** A BAD duration = re-synthesize
  that chapter. VM restarts corrupt in-flight raw files — never trust an unverified raw.
- Probe progress quietly: `grep -E "^\[" <progress_log> | tail -2` + chapter-dir count.

### Step 3 — THE POISON-CHUNK FAILURE MODE (know this before you need it)
Full evidence: `~/workspace/audiobooks/diag/e06_poison_report.md` and
`~/workspace/audiobooks/diag/mudfix/completion_report.md`.

Failure signature: `TTS backend returned truncated or empty audio (26 bytes)` — or,
worse, **silent success with dropped text**.

What it is:
1. Certain EXACT strings deterministically poison the backend **for a given voice**.
   It is per exact string, not per pattern: short lines, quoted questions, and name
   fragments pass all over healthy chapters. Confirmed triggers include a standalone
   short quoted question, repeated speech tags ("Sashko asked."), bare name fragments,
   transliterated sentences, full names inside longer lines, and one combinatorial
   line whose every sentence group passes solo but whose whole fails.
2. Whole-chapter synthesis fails on the poison chunk; the error reports a backend
   chunk index = the 0-based script line index (adjust for >1200-char line splits).
3. **SILENT-DROP WARNING:** in single-line script mode, synthesize-script can report
   SUCCESS while silently dropping the poison sentence **and everything after it**.
   A "successful" repair file is guilty until transcription proves it innocent.

The cure, in order:
1. **Bisect** to the exact line using the reported chunk index; confirm by solo
   synthesis failure.
2. **Re-chunk, never re-word.** Merge the poison line into an adjacent SAME-SPEAKER
   line (backward merge preferred; never forward-merge a dateline/heading line).
   Dropping the duplicate speaker tag at the join is the only permitted token change —
   verify with a word-token diff against the original script: exact match required.
3. Merges work only sometimes. If a merge fails, use **carrier extraction**:
   synthesize a carrier passage that embeds the target sentence mid-sentence (long
   em-dash lead-ins work for full names), whisper-verify the target sentence is
   actually present in the carrier audio, cut it out at silence midpoints, and splice
   it into the chapter assembly.
4. **Transcription-verify EVERY repair** (faster-whisper, see 04_TOOLS_AND_CONNECTIONS)
   — in the FINAL chapter file, not just the repair piece. Dropped-target files get
   caught and discarded at this step; 8 were caught in one book's repair pass.

### Step 4 — Finishing chain
1. Per chapter: loudnorm **I=-16, TP=-1.5, LRA=11** → **44.1 kHz stereo, 96 kbps**.
2. Combine chapters into the full audiobook ONLY when ALL chapters are final.
   Expect ~+1.0s of MP3 concat padding in the combined duration — normal.
3. Spot-check loudness (60s window volumedetect/astats) against sibling chapters for
   consistency.
4. Deliver (Part D).

## PART D — THE MUSIC-BED REMIX (audiobook MP4s/MP3s)

Standing rule: audiobook deliverables ALWAYS carry faint background music under the
narration — never bare voice/silence. Bed tracks live in
`~/workspace/audiobooks/beds/` (primary: `bed_ambient_extended.mp3`, looped).

### The APPROVED chain (Kenneth approved the proof level Oct 31 2026 — use EXACTLY)
1. Bed looped across the full narration span.
2. Bed pre-gain **×0.10**.
3. `sidechaincompress` keyed on the narration: **threshold 0.015, ratio 6,
   attack 20 ms, release 400 ms**.
4. Limiter **0.95**.
5. ⚠️ Do NOT substitute the legacy cover-video variant (threshold 0.03) — it was never
   verified against the approved proof. A bedded file made with any other chain does
   not count as done until re-verified against this chain.

### Verification (all four checks before delivery)
1. Output duration = source duration exactly (Δ 0.00s standard).
2. Speech windows: bedded vs source RMS within ~±0.03 dB (narration fully on top).
3. Longest pause: silence (≈ −70 dB) becomes bed alone (≈ −44 dB, ~24 dB under speech).
4. Cover MP4 variant: video stream copied bit-identical; the bedded MP3 is the ONLY
   audio stream (re-encode to AAC 128k for the MP4); exactly 2 streams total.

### MP4 AUDIOBOOK LAW (Kenneth, Nov 3 2026 — "This is law for MP4 audiobooks")

The MP4 companion to an audiobook SHOWS THE BOOK as it is read — storybook style,
never a static image sitting under narration:

1. **Page-for-page:** each page's own generated image is on screen while that page is
   narrated; when the reading moves to the next page, the picture changes to what's
   happening on that page. Every page gets its own image — none skipped, none reused
   in another page's slot.
2. **As printed:** pages appear EXACTLY as they are in the compiled book (the page's
   art + its typeset text), so a child holding the book can follow along word for
   word. Same order, nothing cropped, nothing re-typeset.
3. **Cover introduction:** the MP4 OPENS on the full front cover (title, series line,
   author) held while the narration's title/intro plays — the book introducing itself,
   just like the other audiobooks open with their intro — then the turn to page 1.
4. **Simple turns:** clean page changes only (plain cuts are perfect) — it is a
   storybook being read, NOT a movie; no fancy motion or effects.
5. **Audio:** the read-aloud/audiobook audio is the video's ONLY audio track,
   unchanged (Part D bed chain already applied to it).
6. **QA before delivery:** frames extracted at every page segment and compared
   against the corresponding compiled-PDF page (same image, same text, legible, full
   page edge to edge); page turns spot-checked against the narration (opening, a
   middle turn, any refrain pages, the closing page); full decode scan clean; total
   duration ≈ the audiobook's duration.

Born from "The Angel Inside You" read-aloud (Nov 3 2026): Kenneth loved the audiobook
and specified this treatment so it can be shared on social. Applies to every future

## LESSONS — Nov 5 2026 — FRANKLIN ILLUSTRATED BUILD (Chasing Candor audiobook)
Production closed Nov 5 2026: bedded MP3 24,475.04s / 293,701,426 B + illustrated Parts (12,444.83s / 690,962,953 B and 12,037.67s / 627,696,279 B, 1080x1920@12fps, faststart), all QA passed, all nine Drive copies byte-verified (Audiobooks / Sell > Audiobooks / franchise hub). Reusable lessons:
1. **ffmpeg inside a read-loop MUST carry `-nostdin`:** a segment encode loop reading segments.tsv from stdin lost its place because ffmpeg consumed the loop's stdin as console commands — one misaligned segment, then failure. `-nostdin` on BOTH ffmpeg call sites in the encode function fixed it permanently. 2. **Restart-kill recovery protocol (proven six times in one night):** when an environment service restart kills a detached build, do NOT trust the newest output file. ffprobe it: a truncated MP4 fails with "moov atom not found." Delete ONLY that file, relaunch the staged script from checkpoint (skip-if-exists preserves everything verified), and note the kill + relaunch in STATUS. 3. **A killed final mux leaves a plausible-looking liar in out/:** the interrupted Part 1 mux sat in out/ at 543 MB of an expected 691 MB with a correct name. Trust only (a) the driver's own DONE line and (b) an ffprobe duration/size check before treating any output as a deliverable. The staged script's mux uses `ffmpeg -y`, so the checkpoint relaunch cleanly overwrites the corpse — no manual cleanup needed, but STATUS must flag the incomplete file so nobody files it mid-flight.
4. **Judge encode/mux/decode progress by bytes, not CPU:** on this box those phases are disk-I/O-bound under fleet contention. The healthy Part 1 mux ran at ~10% CPU writing ~5 MB/min while siblings saturated the disk (vmstat wa ~52%), and the SAME mux on a quiet post-restart disk ran ~70 MB/min. Progress signals that tell the truth: output file size growth between polls, and the reader position in /proc/<pid>/fdinfo for decode scans (93% read = nearly done, whatever the clock says). A low CPU% is not a stall; a frozen file size across two polls is.
5. **Long monitor sleeps get SIGTERM'd by the same instability that kills builds:** poll detached builds with short sleeps (<= ~280s) and treat a killed poll as a prompt to check the build, not as evidence about it. The build is setsid-detached and survives poll deaths; verify the driver process itself before concluding anything.

### LAW 2 — THE PRODUCTION METHOD (Kenneth's own teaching)
Page generation and regeneration run ONE way: upload a copy of the book (the TXT) to the GPT, paste in the Comic Book Maker operational instructions, and direct it page by page — "make the next page, make the next page, make the next page." The book text sitting in the session is what keeps the story exactly the same, just in comic book form. No page is made from memory, summary, or invention when the book itself can sit in the session.

### LAW 3 — THE COMIC PROOFREADER SEAT
A named proofreader sits in the comic gate chain AFTER lettering and BEFORE Liz's final check, on every issue, every page. The pass is twofold: (1) WORD-BY-WORD proofread of everything lettered — captions, balloons, narration boxes, sound words, titles, page furniture — spelling, punctuation, grammar, and the lettered text against the frozen script AND the book (a correctly spelled word that is not the book's word is still a defect); (2) THE NOOKS-AND-CRANNIES SWEEP — balloon tails on the wrong speaker, lettering overflow or crowding, stray marks, broken borders, wrong page numbers, art artifacts in the lettering space. Findings are filed per page and panel; a page carrying a defect REGENERATES from the proofreader's findings before the issue closes — the proofreader's pass decides a regeneration, not a reader's eye after print. No issue is called finished without the proofreader's clean pass on the final bytes. PURPOSE, in Kenneth's words: so we actually have a perfect comic book.
