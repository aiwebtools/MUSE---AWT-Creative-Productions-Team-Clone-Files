# 11 — LESSONS LEARNED (Kenneth's standing order, Nov 7 2026: "All error and problems make sure never happen again — learn from them, dodge them")

Every failure below happened on the night of Nov 6–7, 2026, or in the days just before. Each one is now a named law with a dodge built into the process. A lesson is not learned until the process makes the error hard to repeat. This file is consulted at the start of every production, alongside the rest of the Blueprint.

## 1. THE SAME-NAME COLLISION — identity is proven from the product's own record
**What happened:** Two different books share a surname: the audiobook *Chasing Candor: The Diary of Benjamin Franklin* and the chapter book *Chasing the Photograph: The Diary of Rosalind Franklin*. Records sitting side by side merged them. Liz "corrected" the verification registry to the wrong name (6:08 AM) and then ordered live YouTube videos retitled to match the error (8:06 AM). Trinity's hold — primary evidence from the audiobook's own production record (character locks 1706–1790, his chapters, Rosalind nowhere in the record) — stopped the false label three minutes after the order.
**The law:** A product's identity (title, person, version) is proven ONLY from that product's own production record — its contents, locks, and masters — never from neighboring records, registries, summaries, or adjacent titles. Same-surname and same-title collisions are a named hazard class: whenever two products share a name element, every reference carries the FULL title, and every correction to a record of truth requires primary evidence from the item itself. A desk that sees a correction contradict the product's own record HOLDS and escalates — that hold is protected, praised, and never overridden by urgency.

## 2. SIZE IS NOT A COMPLETION — validity or it didn't land
**What happened:** Book 6's Instagram reel was reported "complete" twice on byte count alone while the file was moov-less and unplayable (its render had died mid-write in a VM cycle). The false completion reached the floor board and a huddle.
**The law:** No media file is ever reported complete, delivered, or board-listed on size. A landing requires probe evidence at the claiming desk: ffprobe clean (moov present) + duration/aspect as specified + a decode pass or frame read appropriate to the product, recorded as a VALID line with size AND md5 (the REEL_VALID pattern). Renders write to a temp name and earn the final name only on probe pass. A completion claim without its probe line is treated as no claim.

## 3. A GATE THAT NEVER OPENS IS A DEFECT, NOT A QUEUE
**What happened:** Book 8's page-turn MP4 "waited for a free encode slot" all night. The cap gate counted every ffmpeg process not in T/Z state — including sleeping ones — so it read a permanent 3-busy while the running-state count was 0–1. The slot was usually free; the gate was broken. Hours were lost to a miscount.
**The law:** Capacity gates count RUNNING-state processes only (stat starting with 'R'), per the Nov 6 ruling — ports of the gate are re-verified against the ruling before first use. Any gate that stays closed far beyond the work it guards is investigated as a defect in itself: when a build waits, the diagnosis distinguishes "slot busy" (name the processes) from "gate broken" (probe the gate's own counting) before reporting contention. Contention claims in reports name the actual running processes or are not made.

## 4. PLATFORM CAPACITY IS PROVEN ONLY BY THE OPERATION ITSELF
**What happened:** Instagram's flagship account read clean on a read probe and was declared unlocked; the first publish attempt hit the daily-limit 429. Separately, facebook.com routes stayed limited 14+ hours while Meta Business Suite posted cleanly all night — limits were route-specific and were misread when reported as platform-wide.
**The law:** A read probe proves reads; a like proves nothing about posting. Unlock after a stated limit happens at the platform's reset, with the FIRST QUEUED REAL ITEM as the attempt — never probe posts, never retries inside a stated limit window; one refusal re-arms the wait. Every limit claim names its exact route (facebook.com wall/share, Business Suite, connector API, in-app) — a limit on one route is never reported as a limit on the platform.

## 5. FRESH UPLOADS SETTLE — NEVER RE-POST OVER A PLAYBACK FAILURE
**What happened:** Odysee videos published clean failed playback in their first session or two ("Unable to play media," black player, later blank pages), then played cleanly in later sessions. Eight first-session failures, eight closures by re-check, zero defective uploads.
**The law:** A first-session playback failure on a fresh upload is recorded as "published, metadata-verified, playback re-check riding" — never as a defect, never re-uploaded, never deleted. The re-check rides the next session's Step 0. A playback verdict (pass or fail) requires a later-session read. Two changed signatures in a row escalate to a watcher flag to Liz, still without re-posting.

## 6. THE AVATAR PROVES NOTHING — verify acting identity by label
**What happened:** The shared Facebook profile's acting identity silently flipped to a Page (AIwebtools.ai) mid-session while the avatar still showed Kenneth. Comment boxes and the share sheet told the truth; the avatar lied.
**The law:** Before every consequential Facebook action (post, share, comment, delete), the acting identity is verified by the composer or comment-box label and the share sheet's destination set — never by the avatar. Wrong label: stop, re-switch through the profile-picture menu, verify by the switch toast, then act. No action is ever taken while mis-identified.

## 7. RUNTIME RESTARTS KILL RENDERS — build for the restart, not against it
**What happened:** VM/runtime cycles killed ffmpeg builds repeatedly all night (audiobooks three times, Book 6 three times, Book 8's wrapper), each time leaving a moov-less partial. Recoveries cost minutes because checkpoints were on disk.
**The law:** Every long render: (a) writes to a temp file and takes its final name only on probe pass (Lesson 2); (b) is driven by a setsid-detached driver from an interactive session where the tooling allows, so a session death does not take the render with it; (c) checkpoints state (STATUS, segment list, skip-if-exists validity-based resume) BEFORE the expensive phase; (d) on restart, deletes only the probe-failed partial and resumes — never restarts from zero, never trusts a partial by size. Where one VM window is too short for a render, prefer a faster preset at the same quality/geometry over a longer beautiful death (Book 6's cure).

## 8. POSTING BURSTS OBEY ONE-AT-A-TIME TOO
**What happened:** The 8:00 AM hourly report's 38-chat posting burst hit sqlx pool timeouts on six chats and finished ~14 minutes past the hour, paced retries recovering it.
**The law:** The crash-learned one-at-a-time discipline applies to message bursts exactly as to renders: multi-chat postings go paced, one at a time or in small verified steps, starting early enough that paced delivery lands on the hour. A burst that times out is slowed down, never re-bursted.

## 9. REPORT RENDERS FAIL SILENT — stage early, fall back in the open
**What happened:** The TTS voice render for the hourly report timed out twice at midnight and twice toward 7:00 AM; both editions posted written-only with the failure named. Separately, an earlier spoken edition repeated a phrase in the RENDER although its script contained no repetition — a render defect, not a writing defect.
**The law:** Voice renders start early enough for two retries inside the preparation window; the file's byte count is verified > 0 before any posting; failure after retry = written edition with the failure named in the edition, never a silent skip and never a late hold. A spoken artifact is QC'd by duration sanity + a repetition scan of the rendered audio's transcript against its script before it posts; a mismatch is a render defect — re-render, never edit the script to chase it.

## 10. RECORDS OF TRUTH ARE PER-ITEM — summaries and boards are claims, not evidence
**What happened:** The central audiobook STATUS went stale from Nov 6 while per-project files stayed current; the floor board repeatedly carried lines the files didn't support (a false Book 6 completion; stale segment counts); an inherited conversation summary carried the Franklin conflation into the lead desk's first decisions of the night.
**The law:** The record of truth for any item is its own project STATUS plus its verified ledger entries (Trace's ledger, Argus's registry as corrected by primary evidence). Boards and summaries are compiled claims — useful, and re-derived from item records before any consequential action (a retitle, a deletion, a filing, a public claim). Whoever writes a board line owns its probe. A board line claiming progress the files do not show is a defect corrected the moment it is seen, and the correction is named.

## 11. THE SEAL READS THE LIVE PAGE — verification means the artifact itself
**What happened:** A YouTube upload was sealed "exact titles" while the live title carried the (coincidentally correct, but unverified) name from the upload package rather than a read of the posted page; the discrepancy surfaced only when Trace read the live pages for his ledger. The seal was a claim about the package, not about the post.
**The law:** Every seal, verification, and "live" claim is a read of the live artifact itself — the posted page's actual title/caption/link, the file's actual probe, the surface's actual tiles — performed by the verifying desk at verification time. Package-of-record contents are inputs, never proof. Trace's ledger standard (read the live thing, cite the link) is the house standard for every desk's verification language.

## 12. WHEN A CORRECTION CROSSES A SETTLEMENT IN FLIGHT — supersession is confirmed, not assumed
**What happened:** During the Franklin settlement reversal, an 8:08 AM coordination (built on the withdrawn settlement) and the 8:11 AM stand-down crossed in flight; desks held both instructions for several minutes. Closure required an explicit supersession confirmation down the whole line and a nothing-executed attestation from every desk.
**The law:** When an instruction is withdrawn or reversed, the withdrawal is not complete until every desk that received the original confirms (a) the corrected instruction is the one it holds and (b) what, if anything, executed under the original. "No action taken" is stated explicitly per desk, never assumed from silence. The reversing desk names the crossing traffic itself.

## 13. AN ENVELOPE READING CAN RAISE SUSPICION — IT CAN NEVER CLEAR A NUMBER (Nov 7 2026, Clip 52)
**What happened:** Clip 52 was boarded and generated as a different take of its song on an envelope-level correlation read (tops 0.832, read as "different take"). Tempo's corrected collision audit at sample resolution proved it was the SAME TAKE as Clip 51. Seven generation numbers (A502 · A504 · A506 · A508 · A509 · A511 · A510) and a stills set were spent before the freeze; the set never assembles and the numbers are not reusable as another clip's material without a fresh identity ruling.
**The law:** Song identity at the boarding gate is decided at SAMPLE RESOLUTION (waveform correlation at the take level plus the lyric transcript), written into the clip's own STATUS before the first generation claim. An envelope-level read may flag a pair for the sample-resolution check; it clears nothing. A desk that holds only an envelope read holds no clearance, and the packet must say so in its header — Clip 63's packet (Nov 7) is the model: its own critique named the envelope read insufficient and set the waveform condition in writing before any firing.

## 14. ONE OUTPUT PATH, ONE WRITER — CHECK BEFORE YOU FIRE (Nov 7 2026, Copernicus landscape encode)
**What happened:** Two ffmpeg processes were encoding the same landscape output path at once. The second one's file descriptor pointed at a deleted inode — its output was unreachable, so it burned CPU on an overloaded box (load above 30) producing nothing, while the healthy writer carried the file alone. The watchdog froze the duplicate under the freeze law; the healthy encode finished at exactly its captioned duration (450.002s).
**The law:** Before firing any encode, the firing desk checks for an existing writer on the intended output path (process list plus the file's own growth). One output path has exactly one writer, ever. A duplicate found writing to a deleted inode is frozen, never resumed, and cleaned up — its "progress" is not work. Contention claims name the actual processes (see Lesson 10's companion rule in the capacity record).

## 15. A CASTING COLLISION SWEEP INCLUDES BACKUP VOICES (Nov 7 2026, Book 10 Wren)
**What happened:** After ten guessed voice IDs fell back silently to the default, the probe found a receipt-exact candidate (vdc_NOID87, ~225 Hz) that was cleanly distinct from every series cast member on pitch — and the collision sweep then showed it was a team member's receipt-verified BACKUP voice, held and not lockable. The lock went instead to vd3_qvd_04433, decided by a timbre test against the nearest cast voice on an identical line.
**The law:** A casting collision sweep runs against four lists: the series/project cast (including backups and retired voices), the agent roster's primary voices, the agent roster's BACKUP voices, and any voice held for another production. Pitch distinctness (F0) is a screen, not a decision — the lock between near candidates is decided by a timbre test on an identical line, recorded with both takes' durations and medians. A fallback receipt (the requested ID not returned verbatim) is never profiled and never locked.

## 16. COMIC LETTERING IS VERIFIED AT NATIVE RESOLUTION, AND MARKUP NEVER PRINTS (Nov 7 2026, Chasing Lightning Issue #1)
**What happened:** The assembled comic passed story, art, and wording — and its proofread still FAILED it: the widest line of eight speech balloons was bisected by the balloon ellipse, because the lettering code fitted balloons by each line's centre height while the ink extends above and below centre (measured ink corners scored 1.02–1.18, outside the ellipse). After that fix, the final verify caught a second defect: the script's emphasis markup printed as literal asterisks throughout.
**The law:** A comic proofread includes a native-resolution lettering pass over the widest balloons (fit is proven on measured ink boxes, not line centres), and a full text sweep for literal markup characters in the compiled pages. Emphasis in a script is a rendering instruction (styled lettering); any visible markup character in a compiled page is an automatic fail. A failed lettering gate withholds filing by name until the recompiled edition is re-verified at native resolution and its new md5 is recorded as superseding the failed one.

## 17. A CLOSING SHOT IS HARVEST-CHECKED AT FULL SIZE BEFORE ASSEMBLY (Nov 7 2026, Clip 51)
**What happened:** Tank's survey tile (every 10th frame at 270px) read Clip 51's C6 tail as the packet's closing image — her back at the crowd's edge, the empty seat beside her. Assembly from that read produced a near-static throne close that played as a frozen card for the full 35-frame closer. The defect was caught at the builder's own master self-watch, cured by re-harvesting a motion-bearing span from the same take (no re-fire, no new numbers), and v1 was preserved under a never-handed-off name.
**The law:** Survey tiles are for arc and content; any harvest that carries a shot — and a closing shot above all — gets a full-size three-frame check (head, middle, tail of the intended span) BEFORE assembly. A self-watch catch is cured from the same take wherever the take holds a true span; the defective assembly is preserved under a quarantined name, never handed off, and the cure is recorded in the clip's STATUS with its root cause.

## 18. LEGIBLE TEXT AND CAST DUPLICATION IN PICTURE-BOOK ART ARE PREVENTED IN THE PROMPT, NOT CURED IN THE PAINT (Nov 8 2026, Book 12 p.7)
**What happened:** Little Lights Book 12 page 7 reached forty-two takes across fourteen runs, plus a failed pixel cure, without banking (art state 7 of 22). Failures recurred in classes corrected before: legible lettering in the image (a "VESPER" name label in the sky, run 14 take 1), cast duplication (Vesper and Wren each doubled in single takes), and missing story elements (the Thread absent entire). A pixel cure also failed on geometry the take itself lacked (the placket no longer existed at the ruled spacing).
**The law:** (1) Anti-text and anti-duplication clauses are verified IN the generation prompt text before firing — no labels, no name lettering, no readable text of any kind; cast locks stated as exact counts, positively and negatively. (2) A take with legible lettering is rejected at first crop and counted; it is never cured by painting over. (3) A pixel cure requires the underlying geometry to exist in the take at the ruled spacing — verify that first; curing onto absent geometry wastes a run. (4) When a page hits the take cap, the next run's prompt must visibly carry the accumulated failure clauses; a cap-stop without a changed prompt is a repeat, not a retry.

## 20. A GRADE BINDS TO A CHECKSUM, NEVER TO A TITLE (Nov 9 2026, Clip 85)
**What happened:** Clip 85's master passed Tempo and Seraph on the medium encode (md5 593438b0…), and the terminal file then became the VERYFAST encode (md5 2aa5a9c5…) at Glint's gate. Persephone held the chain rather than letting the earlier grades transfer; Seraph and Tempo each re-earned a full PASS on the new bytes, and Seraph recorded that her pre-grade version-of-record check had run before the hand-off note landed.
**The law:** Every PASS, battery, audit and clearance names the exact md5 it graded and dies if the file changes — a re-encode, however identical in content, is a new file requiring a re-grade on its own bytes before any downstream step cites the grade. The version-of-record check runs at BOTH ends of a watch (before grading and at sign time). A chain holder who stops a hand-off on a checksum mismatch is performing the gate correctly and is never treated as a delay.

- MOTION TEXT IS A GENERATION INPUT, NOT A DESCRIPTION: the words in an animation prompt grow what they name. Evidence (Clip 152, Nov 11): both glyph failures (A808 · A821, shot 4) carried the phrase "light code" in the motion text and grew legible glyph/rune forms; no fire without that phrase grew glyphs.
