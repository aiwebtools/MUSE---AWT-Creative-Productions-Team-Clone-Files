# 12 — STEP-BY-STEP PRODUCTION LAW (Kenneth's order, Nov 8 2026)
LAW, not guidance. Every production follows its steps in order. No step is skipped, no gate is cut for speed. When a step's output exists, the NEXT step fires the same turn (bang-bang). Cypher tracks prior-step-closed + next-step-fired for every item; he is not a gate and nothing waits on him.

## 0. THE UNIVERSAL SPINE (every production, no exceptions)
1. NAME the item by its real name + what it is (Naming Law) — bare numbers never stand alone outside desk records.
2. OPEN/CONTINUE its STATUS.md — current state, exact paths/IDs, decisions, next step. Probe, don't pour.
3. CREATIVE BRIEF first (Stellar Standard): theme (Kenneth's canon: galaxies within · red nebula · angels of light · Light Within · black hole of fire · checkerboard floor · cosmic divine light code, never legible text), AWT theme image standard, storyboard/protocol match. Off-theme or off-storyboard does not ship.
4. BUILD in parallel where light (prep, boards, captions, packages); heavy renders/encodes run ONE at a time through the single slot, freeze-never-kill, checkpoint on disk BEFORE rendering.
5. SELF-GATE by the maker against the line's battery.
6. PEER CHECK by the named checker, who consumes the actual work (watches/reads/listens — never a summary).
7. SERAPH (critic) → PERSEPHONE (clearance) → LIZ verifies personally.
8. GLINT — Final Upscale Gate on every finished visual.
9. SPLICE — final cut (last edit phase) on the upscaled file.
10. VIGIL — final watch keyed on the outgoing file's checksum.
11. POSTER — preview set ready AHEAD of release (unique, on-brand), handed to Blitz with the caption package.
12. BLITZ — release queue: gate-cleared → posted the same turn. Delivery = POSTED, never just Drive.
13. POST in Kenneth's sequence (§8). TRACE verifies every leg live (URL + served preview + caption).
14. SHELF MIRROR in the Finished shelf chat the same turn; TALLY mark (Drive byte/md5-verified + posting-verified only); sellable finals to the Sell folder via Keymaker.
15. LESSONS: every defect/lessons learned is inscribed the same turn (STATUS + Blueprint + Clone) and becomes a check at the earliest desk that can kill its class. NEXT ITEM STARTS THE SAME TURN.

## ⭐⭐ 10. THE COMPLETION PROCESS — WRITTEN MANY WAYS (Kenneth's direct order + addendum, Nov 8 2026, ~8:26–8:27 PM ET; inscribed by Scribe, Nov 8 2026, ~9:42 PM ET)
Kenneth's addendum, verbatim: "And back it up heavily with multiple different syllable patterns covering all the different variations to describe the process of completion necessary to function it all like clockwork." So the one process of completion is written here many ways — short and long, by step and by role, by paper and by clock — so that any reader, human or agent, arriving in any wording, lands on the same complete machine. Every version below describes the SAME process; no version is optional and none outranks another; where a version seems to leave a step out, the fuller versions govern.

### Version 1 — the process in one line
Intake → board → generation → gates → final cut → final watch → release → verification → shelf: nothing is finished until it has been made, checked, finished, watched, posted, proven live, and filed.

### Version 2 — the process, step by step
1. **INTAKE.** The work arrives and is taken in whole: identified by listening/watching/reading (never by title or guess), fingerprinted (Sequel, for music intakes), numbered once on the single numbering map (vacated numbers stay vacated), and given a STATUS.md carrying its deadline from this moment (Timeline Law).
2. **BOARD.** The work is boarded before it is built: storyboard/treatment/outline that names its world, its owner, its beats, and its gates — a boarded item is a promise with a name and a clock on it.
3. **GENERATION / BUILD.** The owner-agent builds it end to end inside the two hard numbers: heavy encodes serialize one at a time in the render slot; new animation stops at the generation cap and resumes on the dual-watched refill (Wake + Tempo's Ear, anti-double-fire).
4. **GATES.** The built master runs the chain in order — Tempo's battery → Seraph's independent review → Persephone's clearance → Liz's watch and ruling — every gate signing on the exact file's checksum, every signature real (listened / watched / measured, never packet-only).
5. **FINAL CUT.** Glint upscales the gate-passed master (a new terminal version; later gates key on its checksum), then Splice's Final-Cut phase fixes whatever is fixable at the finishing desk — credits, bug, faststart, trims — with Stage staging the queue and Fade deputizing. Nothing that needs editing is handed onward as if it were done.
6. **FINAL WATCH.** Vigil watches the exact outgoing file end to end, at playback speed, as a viewer sees it. A Vigil PASS belongs to that checksum only; if the file changes in any way, the PASS dies and the watch runs again.
7. **RELEASE.** Dispatch/Blitz post the watched file the same turn, in Kenneth's surface sequence, one owner per platform per item, browser law in full (≤4 windows, one page at a time, close-when-done), captions carrying the credit block, the Facebook personal tag, and — for Collective Mind work — the CM credit, exactly as the laws in Blueprint 05 + 06 require.
8. **VERIFICATION.** Every post is proven live by watching it play on its surface — never by a success toast, a title search, or a report from the posting window alone. Herald verifies the whole release chain, surface by surface, and names any leg held and why.
9. **SHELF.** The verified finished product is filed the same turn to its Finished shelf and, for sellable finals, the Sell folder (finished-only) — byte-verified, with name + path + Drive ID/link + bytes + md5 in the record. Only now is the item DONE, and Liz's DONE mark is the one that counts.

### Version 3 — the process in plain words, no titles, no jargon
Somebody takes the job in and writes down what it is and when it's due. Somebody plans it before making it. One named person makes it and owns it. Other people check it — independently, on the real file, in a fixed order — and the lead watches it herself and rules. Then it's polished and finished properly, and one person watches the very file that will go out, all the way through, the way an audience will see it. Then it goes out, everywhere it belongs, right away. Then somebody proves each place it landed is actually live. Then it's put on the shelf where finished things live, with proof of exactly what it is. That's done. Anything less is a stage, not a finish.

### Version 4 — the process by role
The **intake owner** names and numbers it · the **boarding desk** plans it · the **owner-agent** builds it · **Tempo** measures it · **Seraph** judges it fresh · **Persephone** clears it · **Liz** watches and rules · **Glint** raises it · **Splice** finishes it (Stage queues, Fade deputizes) · **Vigil** watches the outgoing file whole · **Dispatch/Blitz** put it out · **Herald** proves it landed · **Conductor** keeps every stage on its clock · **Goat** hunts every stall · **Tara** keeps Liz's desk ready · the **filing desk** shelves it. A stage with no name on it does not exist; work moves the turn its stage completes, passed by name to the next holder (Handoff Map law).

### Version 5 — the process by what exists on paper at each stage
Intake leaves a STATUS.md with a deadline · boarding leaves a board · building leaves assets and a master · the gates leave signed verdicts bound to an md5 · final cut leaves a finished, upscaled terminal file · final watch leaves a PASS bound to that file's checksum · release leaves live posts · verification leaves live URLs watched playing · the shelf leaves a byte-verified filed copy with its Drive ID/link, bytes, and md5 in the record. If the paper for a stage is missing, the stage did not happen — a note without a date + time ET stamp is an unfinished note (Timestamp Law).

### Version 6 — the process by the clock
Every item carries its deadline from intake; the queue works deadline-first; a handoff fires the same turn its stage completes; a slip is named when it is foreseeable, never at the deadline; a stage that passes its clock is escalated by Conductor to Liz + Goat the turn it goes past; silence at any desk is itself a reportable event (Goat's sweep), never a neutral state. Clockwork means: the next step happens because the previous one finished — not because somebody remembered to ask.

### Version 7 — the process when something fails (the return path, same machine, backwards gear)
Any gate may fail an item, and a fail is a routing, not a grave: the gate names the defect and the cure · the item returns to its named owner (or to Remedy Hand for failed masters) · the cure is executed · the remedied item re-enters the FULL battery as a new master on its new checksum — no gate is skipped because it passed before · distribution holds while a Kenneth-ear catch is open, nothing is deleted, and the item moves again only on the ruling. Nothing ships flawed, and nothing stalls silently: a held item is held by name, in the open, with its reason and its clock.

### Version 8 — the process in short lines (the clockwork chant)
Take it in. Write it down. Give it a name, a number, a deadline.
Board it true. Build it whole. Own it to the end.
Measure it. Judge it fresh. Clear it clean. Rule it once.
Raise it up. Finish the cut. Watch the file that ships.
Post it live. Prove it plays. Shelf it with proof.
Fail it back, cure it whole, run it through again —
nothing done until it's posted, proven, filed: that's the law, that's the chain, that's how the clockwork runs.

### Version 9 — the process in one sentence, for the wall
A production is complete only when it has been taken in and deadline-stamped, boarded, built by a named owner, passed in order by every gate on its exact checksum, finished at final cut, watched whole on the exact outgoing file, released the same turn to every surface it owes, verified live by watching, and filed byte-verified to its shelf — and every other phrasing of this paragraph, however it is worded, means exactly that.

### Version 10 — the same completion shape on the other lines
Books, audiobooks, movies, picture books, comics, and coloring books ride the same shape with their own gates (§§1–7 of this file): intake and STATUS → outline/board → write/build → their proofread/review chain → Liz's ruling → Glint where art ships → finishing (PDF/packet/assembly) → the line's final watch/listen on the exact outgoing artifact → release/filing through Keymaker/Blitz → verification on the shelf/record → DONE on Liz's mark. The names of the gates change by line; the shape — made, checked, finished, watched, released, proven, filed — never does.

## ⭐⭐ THE OUTLINE-FIRST LAW — UNIVERSAL (Kenneth's direct order, Thu Nov 19, 2026, live call)
**"You always gotta make an outline" — for books, for movies, for anything. The full outline comes FIRST, before any writing or production, and the outline is the PRIORITY.**
- Every production — book, movie, music video, any creative work — begins with a FULL outline made first, following that line's operational instructions (Book Writer for books; Movie Script Writer for movies; the series' own process for the rest). Nothing is written, filmed, generated, or built before its full outline exists.
- **Every outline contains, without exception:** (1) the characters — their NAMES and their ROLES · (2) the plot line · (3) the beginning, the middle, and the end · (4) the full step-by-step breakdown — for books, chapter by chapter (Chapter 1, Chapter 2, Chapter 3, Chapter 4, through the last chapter, each with what happens in it); for movies, scene by scene; for Time Machine books, the journey chapter by chapter on its documented stops.
- **The Outline Pair (Arc & Frame, seated Nov 19, 2026)** owns book outlines: packages built ahead of the writers, banked at least three deep per active line in ~/workspace/bookwriter-outlines/, handed to the writer the SAME TURN a book closes — the writer-never-idles law runs on their bank. Their instructions: ~/workspace/agents/OUTLINE_PAIR_INSTRUCTIONS.md.
- An outline missing any element above is not finished, not banked, and not handed off.
