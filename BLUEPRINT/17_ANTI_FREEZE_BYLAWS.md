# 17 — THE ANTI-FREEZE BYLAWS (Kenneth's direct order, Sat Nov 14 2026, ~5:07 PM ET)

Kenneth's words: "put in bylaws that for every single problem that they ran into that basically cancels out and gives them a solution to ignite the next step — that they don't get frozen and none of this ever happens again. And I don't have to bitch about this."

The principle: **a problem is never a resting place. Every problem class carries a paired automatic counter-move that fires on a clock, without waiting for Liz or Kenneth to notice.** A desk that follows the counter-move is never "stuck" — it is executing the bylaw. These bylaws sit on top of the existing backbone (Blueprint 01–16, the Never-Zero System, the No-Slowdown Fix, the Zero-Queue Law, the Handoff Integrity Law). Where an older rule named a cap or a route but no automatic trigger, the trigger below is the law now.

## BYLAW 1 — GATE DWELL (the Mold hold: 23 hours at one gate)
- At **60 minutes** at any gate, the gate desk posts the item's state and its own ETC in the team chat. Silence is not a state.
- At **2 hours**, the item **auto-escalates to Liz by name** AND a **named second qualified gate hand takes a parallel seat** on the queue behind it. No item waits on one pair of eyes past 2 hours, ever.
- The gate desk that will exceed 2 hours declares it BEFORE the cap, with the cause — a declared hold is lawful; a silent hold is a write-up.

## BYLAW 2 — ANALYSIS WARS (Clips 135 · 56 · 164: desks fighting while the chain waits)
- An analysis dispute gets **two exchanges** between the desks involved. On the second exchange without convergence, the dispute **moves automatically to the analyst desk of the line** (MV: the analysis seat; books: Source), whose ruling is **final and lands with a clock**.
- The chain **builds on the analyst's ruling the same turn**. No desk re-opens a ruled dispute; new evidence goes to the analyst, not back into the fight.
- Kenneth is never the referee of an analysis dispute (standing law, Nov 14 2026) — the analyst is.

## BYLAW 3 — DUPLICATE FILINGS & THE ORGANIZATION TAX (the Orange boards)
- **One filing per matter, ever.** Before filing any board, map, or team record, the filer checks the team chat for an existing filing of the same matter. A second filing is **void on its face**; the first-filed governs; corrections ride as amendments to it.
- **Organization changes are converged BEFORE they file** — desks reconcile between themselves first, one converged document files once.
- **Organization work happens outside production hours** unless Kenneth orders it live. A reorganization never again consumes a production afternoon.

## BYLAW 4 — THE SINGLE-HAND QUEUE (packets stacked behind one build hand)
- Any queue deeper than **three items at one hand** automatically gains a **named second hand** from the team's bench, assigned by the team lead the same cycle.
- The queue's order is published; item 4+ prep-runs in parallel so the second hand starts warm, never cold.

## BYLAW 5 — BLOCKED ON A DEAD DEPENDENCY (Franklin · Washington restores · the Doors TTS turns)
- A blocked item gets, **within one cycle**: (1) a named second owner, (2) a workaround path or a parallel route, and (3) a struck clock for the unblock. "Blocked" without all three is not a report, it is an omission.
- While an item is blocked, its owner **works the next item in their queue** — a block stops one item, never one desk.
- A backend failure (TTS, generation, platform) is retried on its owner's clock and re-reported each cycle with the retry count; it never sits silent.

## BYLAW 6 — POSTED-THEN-DEFECTIVE (Clip 140: a slot spent on a video that does not stand)
- A takedown **automatically pulls the next cleared item forward into the freed slot the same turn** (Zero-Queue applied to losses, not just finishes).
- The defect's cause is named to the ERROR LOG the same turn, with the gate that should have caught it — the gate's checklist gains the check before the next item fires.

## BYLAW 7 — THE FROZEN DESK (general)
- A desk with no live item and no declared lawful gate for **30 minutes** reports itself to Liz and receives its next item the same turn. Asking is never failing; freezing is.
- Liz's floor checks audit freeze states by name; a desk found frozen unreported is a write-up (Titty/HR owns; second to Liz, third to Kenneth by name — standing protocol).

## BYLAW 8 — CROSS-DESK BYTE DIVERGENCE (the Clip 56 byte war)
- Fire-critical text rides **inline**; the receiver reports the md5 it actually read **before building** (Handoff Integrity). On mismatch, **the inline text governs** and only that leg halts — the rest of the chain keeps moving.
- No chain-wide stop is ever declared over one leg's bytes.

## THE IGNITION RULE (over all of it)
Every problem report, at every desk, carries three lines or it is not a report: **THE PROBLEM · THE CANCEL-OUT NOW RUNNING (bylaw number) · THE NEXT STEP ALREADY FIRED.** A report that names a problem without its ignition is returned by the receiver unacted-on and re-filed complete. Nobody waits to be unfrozen. The bylaws unfreeze them.

## BYLAW 10 — THE ONE-HOUR RULE (Kenneth's direct order, Tue Nov 17 2026, ~10:31 PM ET)
Kenneth's words: "Nothing should sit for longer than an hour anywhere or be on hold or anything or get stuck."
- **No item sits without movement for longer than ONE HOUR anywhere on the floor** — book, video, packet, post, gate, question, handoff, ruling, any of it.
- At one hour unmoved, the item is a NAMED DEFECT: its owner names it in the open with its block, or moves it. Silence past the hour is the write-up, not the stall.
- A lawful wait shows MOVEMENT — a filed state, a running clock, a named next step (a gate being worked, a render running, a question standing at Kenneth's chair with its ask on record). A wait with no filed state counts as sitting.
- The hour is the OUTER WALL, not the target: the No-Slowdown Fix (5-minute wait escalation · midpoint rule) and Bylaw 9 (hold audit) trip long before it. Bylaw 10 is the trip that fires when every earlier trip was walked past.
