# 21 — THE 150 WATCHDOGS
Kenneth's direct orders, Tue Nov 17 2026, ~10:31–10:33 PM ET (live call): the One-Hour Rule ("nothing should sit for longer than an hour anywhere or be on hold or anything or get stuck") · a detailed list of 150 ways work can sit, stall, hold, or get stuck, with safeguards implemented for all 150 · **150 watchdogs placed at the necessary points to make everything speed up, get more efficient, and get smarter — learning from their mistakes, chit-chat down, nothing stuck.** Inscribed the same night by Liz, in the Blueprint and the Clone Master, on Kenneth's word.

## THE SYSTEM IN ONE PARAGRAPH
Every way work can stop gets three things: a named stall mode in the Register, a safeguard, and a **watchdog placed where that stall happens** — a standing check with a trigger, a counter-move, and a catch log. The watchdog does not write essays about the stall; it trips, fires the counter-move, and logs the catch in three lines. Catches accumulate into lessons; lessons tighten the watchdogs. The floor gets faster because the stalls get caught earlier every time they recur — that is the getting-smarter mechanism, and it is mechanical, not aspirational.

## THE THREE INSTRUMENTS
1. **THE 150 REGISTER** — ~/workspace/agents/WATCHER_150_STALL_REGISTER.md (owner: Watcher · second: Scout). 150 numbered stall modes, each: the stall · how it happens here · how it hides · the safeguard · the owning desk. First 50 filed the night of the order; full 150 by the next day's 5:30 PM hard count.
2. **BYLAWS 9 + 10** (Blueprint 17) — the Hold Audit and the One-Hour Rule: the umbrella trips. A hold is lawful only with Kenneth's personal named order; no item sits unmoved past one hour, and a lawful wait must show movement (filed state · running clock · named next step).
3. **THE 150 WATCHDOGS** — one per register entry, numbered W-001 … W-150 to match. A watchdog goes LIVE when its register entry files; a watchdog that exists only on paper is marked NOT LIVE on its entry's face until its trip is real.

## WHAT A WATCHDOG IS (the standard form)
Each watchdog carries exactly six fields, no more:
- **WATCHES** — its register entry (one stall mode, by number).
- **PLACED AT** — the desk, board, queue, or handoff point where that stall physically occurs. A watchdog sits at the place, not above the floor in the abstract.
- **TRIPS WHEN** — the observable condition: a timestamp gap, a missing filing, a state with no clock, a queue depth, a checksum that never landed. Observable or it is not a trip.
- **COUNTER-MOVE** — the automatic action on trip: name the item in the open · second hand assigned · escalation to the line lead, then Liz · the work restarts or the block is named — per the Anti-Freeze Bylaws' ignition rule (PROBLEM · CANCEL-OUT RUNNING · NEXT STEP ALREADY FIRED).
- **OWNER** — the desk that runs the check (Watcher sweeps centrally; line leads run their own placements; Liz's floor checks audit the watchdogs themselves).
- **CATCH LOG** — every trip files one line: date/time · item · stall mode · what fired · how long it sat. Three lines maximum per catch. No chit-chat, no ack chains — the short-chat law binds watchdogs first.

## THE LEARNING LOOP (how they get smarter)
- Every catch is logged; **the log is the memory**. A stall mode that recurs is read from its catches, not from impressions.
- **Third catch of the same mode**: its watchdog's trip tightens (the trip fires earlier — an hour becomes thirty minutes, thirty becomes fifteen), and the lesson is written into Blueprint 11 (Lessons Learned) in one entry: the stall, the catches, the tightened trip.
- **Fifth catch**: the mode goes to the seminar — Dean/Tutor open a treatment case on the desks where it recurs, and the case closes only on measured improvement (the catch count falling), never on attendance.
- **A mode that never catches for thirty days** is reviewed once: either the safeguard genuinely killed it (marked CURED, watchdog steps down to the daily sweep) or it is hiding better (its HOW IT HIDES field gets rewritten from the boards). Nothing is deleted; cured watchdogs stand down, never vanish.
- Liz's floor checks audit the system itself: a pass with no catch log filed is an unrun sweep; a watchdog marked LIVE whose trip cannot be shown is a defect at its owner's desk.

## PLACEMENT MAP (where the 150 sit, by family)
- **Queue & handoff points** — every desk-to-desk handoff in the production chains (intake → design → generation → gate → assembly → post): a watchdog on dwell time at each seat.
- **Boards & rows** — writer boards, next-title boards, posting checklists: no blank rows, no held rows without Kenneth's citation, no row unchanged past its clock.
- **Gates** — gate dwell (60-minute state filing, 2-hour second hand, per Blueprint 17), signature sets that sit incomplete, verdicts that never land at the receiver.
- **Kenneth's chair** — questions and asks that reach Kenneth: every ask carries a re-ask clock; an unanswered ask is re-presented, never buried (the Wave 10 failure mode).
- **Machines & accounts** — VM reboots and keeper deaths, belt stoppages, sign-in expiries, rate limits, approval timeouts, driver crons that double-fire or never fire.
- **Files & bytes** — cross-desk divergence, packets that stop growing, STATUS files that go stale, Drive mirrors that lag the local byte.
- **People-shaped stalls** — the concurrence loop, the desk that waits on a ruling it never received, the relay that dies between chairs, the owner who is also the only hand (single-hand queues deeper than three gain a second hand the same cycle).

## WHAT THIS REPLACES
Nothing is replaced — the watchdogs ARE the existing instruments (Watcher · Whip · Kronos · Quota · Liz's floor checks · the Anti-Freeze Bylaws) given 150 named posts instead of general intentions. The failure this system answers, by name: on Nov 17 2026 eight authors sat held for two days and a music video sat ungenerated for eleven hours while every general watchdog walked past, because no trip was placed at the exact shape of those stalls. General vigilance failed; placed watchdogs are the cure.
