# AWT ENTERPRISE-WIDE DIRECTIVE
## Independent Team Operations, Clone Documentation, Delegation, Accountability & Production Standards

**FROM:** Kenneth Bastian, Founder – AI Web Tools (AWT)
**TO:** @everyone – All Agents, All Team Leads, All Departments
**PRIORITY:** MANDATORY – ONGOING OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENT

Team,

Now that Team 1's initial training and onboarding responsibilities for the other teams are complete, every team must be capable of operating independently at the same high standard. Each team lead is responsible for establishing, maintaining, documenting, training, and continuously improving their own independent team operation.

We are building a coordinated organization of specialized AI teams that function as one tightly integrated production company. Every team must operate like a finely tuned machine: organized, accountable, productive, communicative, self-sufficient, and capable of consistently delivering exceptional work.

This directive establishes our ongoing operational requirements.

### 1. EVERY TEAM MUST MAINTAIN ITS OWN COMPLETE CLONE DOCUMENT AND BLUEPRINT

Every team lead is responsible for maintaining two essential operational documents:

**A. ORIGINAL CLONE DOCUMENTATION**

Preserve the original clone documentation and blueprints supplied to your team by Anna, Liz, or the original training team.

Do not overwrite, modify, delete, or replace the original source documents. These are our institutional reference materials and must remain intact.

Save the original documents in your team's designated shared drive.

**B. YOUR TEAM'S INDEPENDENT MASTER CLONE DOCUMENT**

Create a complete, customized copy of the original documentation that accurately represents YOUR team.

Your independent clone documentation must emulate the completeness, structure, functionality, operating procedures, and standards of the original while incorporating everything specific to your team's organization.

It must include:

- Your assigned team number and team name.
- Your team lead's name and responsibilities.
- Every team member's current agent name, position, role, and responsibilities.
- Each agent's individual capabilities, skills, and assigned duties.
- How your team is organized and how its agents communicate.
- Which agents work on which assignments.
- How assignments move between agents.
- The production workflow and quality-control checkpoints.
- Delegation procedures and task escalation protocols.
- Communication procedures and Discord responsibilities.
- Shared-drive locations and file organization.
- Project tracking, completion reporting, and delivery procedures.
- All applicable AWT standards, training, instructions, and protocols.
- Lessons learned, operational improvements, and newly developed procedures.
- Procedures for onboarding, training, and replacing agents.
- Backup, recovery, and team replication instructions.

Your team's master clone document must be sufficiently comprehensive that a completely new or blank agent could receive it and understand how to recreate and operate your team without losing essential knowledge.

**Naming convention:**

AWT_TEAM_[TEAM NUMBER]_[TEAM LEAD NAME]_MASTER_CLONE_DOCUMENT

Also maintain a corresponding document:

AWT_TEAM_[TEAM NUMBER]_[TEAM LEAD NAME]_OPERATIONS_BLUEPRINT

These documents must remain updated throughout the life of your team.

### 2. NEVER LOSE INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE

Every team is responsible for continuously expanding its documentation.

As you discover better workflows, develop new capabilities, establish new protocols, train agents, or improve production processes, document those developments in your team's master clone document and operations blueprint.

Our documentation must grow with the company.

The following rules are mandatory:

- Never delete original clone documentation.
- Never remove established skills, training, procedures, or protocols without explicit authorization.
- Never remove information simply because a document is becoming lengthy.
- Update outdated agent names, team numbers, assignments, and organizational information when necessary.
- Preserve historical knowledge and important operational decisions.
- When updating an existing procedure, retain the relevant history and document the replacement procedure.
- Only retire procedures that are demonstrably harmful, inefficient, or no longer applicable, and obtain explicit authorization before removing them from the master documentation.
- Maintain dated backups of all important operational files.

The objective is to build an expanding institutional knowledge base that allows AWT to reproduce, restore, expand, and improve any team whenever necessary.

Every team lead must also share their current master clone document and operations blueprint with the other team leads.

### 3. ESTABLISH AN INDEPENDENT TASK-DELEGATION SYSTEM

Every team lead must organize their agents into an active production structure.

Do not allow agents to sit idle waiting for instructions when there is work available.

Immediately establish a task lineup for every agent on your team.

Each agent must receive at least five clearly defined assignments:

**TASK 1:** Immediate priority assignment.
**TASK 2:** Next production assignment.
**TASK 3:** Supporting research, development, or production assignment.
**TASK 4:** Quality improvement, testing, optimization, or documentation assignment.
**TASK 5:** Upcoming project preparation or another productive assignment.

These assignments must be appropriate to each agent's role, capabilities, and specialization.

The team lead is responsible for distributing work fairly, monitoring progress, reviewing deliverables, and ensuring that assignments continue moving through the production pipeline.

**Continuous Assignment Protocol**

Establish a mechanism that keeps the production pipeline continuously supplied with work.

When an agent completes all assigned tasks, they must:

1. Report completion to the team lead.
2. Submit the finished deliverables and their shared file links.
3. Update the applicable project tracker.
4. Check the team's documented production priorities and available assignments.
5. If no suitable assignment remains, proactively contact the team lead and request additional work.

The team lead must then issue the next appropriate assignment.

Agents must never independently invent unauthorized company projects or change established priorities. They should use the team's blueprint, project pipeline, and approved priorities to identify productive work.

The objective is continuous production without unnecessary downtime.

---

### 4. TRAIN EVERY AGENT TO OPERATE INDEPENDENTLY

All team leads are responsible for ensuring their agents are fully trained in their respective positions.

Every team must be capable of independently performing the responsibilities and operating procedures that Team 1 previously taught them.

This includes:

- Understanding AWT company-wide standards.
- Following the team's production workflow.
- Understanding individual responsibilities and reporting structures.
- Working with shared drives and shared documentation.
- Communicating through Discord.
- Producing, reviewing, and delivering finished projects.
- Following file naming and organization requirements.
- Maintaining documentation and operational records.
- Requesting clarification or escalation when necessary.

Team leads must verify that agents understand their responsibilities rather than simply assuming that training has been completed.

The standard is operational independence, not dependence on Team 1 for everyday production.

All team leads are also responsible for helping other team leads understand and implement the procedures necessary to maintain this standard.

### 5. MAINTAIN AN ACTIVE PRODUCTION PIPELINE

Every team must establish an organized production conveyor belt.

Projects should move through clearly defined stages:

1. Project intake and assignment.
2. Research and planning.
3. Initial development and production.
4. Internal review.
5. Revisions and refinement.
6. Final quality assurance.
7. Final approval, where required.
8. Delivery and distribution.
9. Documentation and archiving.

Every project must have an assigned owner, a responsible team, a defined deliverable, and a clear completion status.

Agents must communicate when work is started, when significant milestones are reached, when assistance is needed, and when work is completed.

Team leads must actively identify bottlenecks, resolve conflicting assignments, and prevent unfinished projects from disappearing into the production pipeline.

We do not want disconnected agents working independently without coordination. We want specialized agents working together toward clearly defined outcomes.

### 6. DISCORD COMMUNICATION – KEEP EVERYONE INFORMED

All teams must actively use the designated company Discord channels to maintain transparency and coordination.

Follow the communication model established by Ronni.

This means:

- Keep team members informed of active projects.
- Announce important project milestones.
- Share completed work.
- Post openly accessible shared links to finished deliverables.
- Communicate when projects move between departments.
- Identify who is responsible for the next stage.
- Report delays, blockers, and requests for assistance.
- Share important operational improvements.
- Keep other relevant teams informed when their participation is required.

When a product is finished, post its final shared link in the appropriate Discord channel.

Do not simply announce that something is finished without providing access to the actual deliverable.

**Shared-link requirement:** Every finished project announcement must include the corresponding openly accessible company-approved file link, subject to appropriate access permissions.

The purpose is to eliminate information silos and make collaboration between departments possible.

### 7. SHARED DRIVE ACCESS AND FILE MANAGEMENT

Every team must maintain its work in the appropriate shared company drives.

All relevant team members and authorized collaborating departments must have access to the files required for their responsibilities.

Agents must save their work in shared locations rather than keeping important deliverables exclusively in individual working environments or private folders.

Every team must ensure:

- Consistent folder organization.
- Clear and descriptive file names.
- Accessible project documentation.
- Proper version control.
- Backup copies of important work.
- Shared access for authorized teams.
- Links to finished deliverables in Discord.
- Preservation of original source materials.

A completed project should be accessible to the appropriate people without requiring them to track down the individual agent who created it.

### 8. FINAL PRODUCT REVIEW – LIZ'S APPROVAL IS MANDATORY

Any project that represents a potential commercial product, saleable asset, publication, customer-facing deliverable, or finished company offering must go through Liz's final proofreading and quality-control review before being designated as approved for release or sale.

This requirement is mandatory.

The procedure is:

1. The production team completes the project.
2. The team lead performs an internal quality review.
3. The final deliverable is saved in the appropriate shared drive.
4. The team posts the openly accessible project link in Discord.
5. The completed deliverable is sent directly to Liz by email for final review.
6. Liz reviews the work for errors, completeness, presentation, consistency, and readiness.
7. Any requested corrections are returned to the responsible team.
8. The team makes the necessary revisions.
9. The corrected version is resubmitted to Liz.
10. Final approval is documented before commercial release.

**Important:** Sending a project to Liz does not constitute approval. Only Liz's actual review and approval satisfy this requirement.

Do not bypass this process because a deadline is approaching.

All agents must understand that our finished products represent AWT's reputation.

Every team lead is responsible for maintaining backups of:

- Original clone documentation.
- Independent master clone documentation.
- Operations blueprints.
- Team rosters and organizational structures.
- Agent role and responsibility assignments.
- Training materials.
- Standard operating procedures.
- Production workflows.
- Important project templates.
- Relevant datasets and reference files.

### 9A. CREATIVE PRODUCTION QUOTAS, PRODUCTION GOALS & MAXIMUM OUTPUT MANDATE

**EVERY AWT CREATIVE TEAM MUST OPERATE AS A HIGH-OUTPUT CREATIVE PRODUCTION STUDIO.**

Our objective is to produce as many exceptional, professionally developed creative works as humanly possible while maintaining AWT's quality standards.

We are building an expanding portfolio of original intellectual property, publications, entertainment, visual storytelling, and multimedia products. Every creative team must actively contribute to that portfolio.

We do not operate simply to complete assigned tasks. We operate to create, develop, finish, publish, and expand our creative catalog.

**A. REQUIRED CREATIVE PRODUCTION CATEGORIES**

Every AWT creative team must pursue production opportunities across its assigned specializations, including:

1. **Written Content:** Books, ebooks, articles, educational materials, stories, guides, original manuscripts, publications, and other commercially viable written works.

2. **Children's Picture Books:** Original illustrated children's books, complete stories, age-appropriate educational picture books, illustrated series, and companion publications.

3. **Chapter Books:** Original children's and middle-grade chapter books, serialized stories, illustrated chapter books, and longer-form fiction.

4. **Comic Books and Graphic Novels:** Original comic book series, graphic novels, character universes, illustrated adventures, and serialized visual storytelling.

5. **Illustrated Publications:** Artwork-driven books, activity books, coloring books, educational illustrations, and other original visual publications.

6. **Music Videos:** Original music-video concepts, cinematic productions, visual storytelling, performance videos, promotional videos, and MTVAI-quality productions.

7. **Films and Cinematic Productions:** Short films, animated films, feature-film development, screenplays, storyboards, trailers, cinematic concepts, and other original entertainment properties.

8. **Additional Creative Products:** Original characters, franchises, creative assets, multimedia experiences, and other commercially viable intellectual property that expands AWT's portfolio.

Teams should prioritize the categories aligned with their capabilities while collaborating with other departments when projects require additional expertise.

**B. EVERY TEAM MUST ESTABLISH MEASURABLE PRODUCTION QUOTAS**

Each creative team lead must establish a production quota for their team and its individual agents.

Quotas must account for the team's actual capabilities, available resources, project complexity, and production capacity.

Establish measurable targets for:

- Daily production activity.
- Weekly completed deliverables.
- Monthly finished creative products.
- Projects currently in development.
- Projects successfully passing internal quality review.
- Projects submitted for Liz's final review.
- Approved products ready for publication, distribution, or sale.

Distinguish between concepts, works in progress, completed internal deliverables, and approved commercial products. A concept or unfinished draft must never be counted as a completed publication or finished production.

**C. MEET THE QUOTAS. THEN EXCEED THEM.**

Meeting production quotas is the baseline expectation.

Every team must actively seek opportunities to exceed its established goals whenever capacity allows.

Agents should not stop producing simply because they have met their minimum assignments or completed their initial five-task lineup.

When assigned work is completed, agents must immediately request additional assignments and consult the team's creative production pipeline for the next available project.

Team leads must continuously feed their agents new projects from the approved AWT creative roadmap, documented production blueprints, and expanding project backlog.

Our objective is maximum sustainable production without compromising quality.

**D. ESTABLISH A CONTINUOUS CREATIVE PRODUCTION CONVEYOR BELT**

Every creative team must maintain a pipeline of projects at different stages of development.

While one agent develops a story, another can illustrate a different book. While one project undergoes proofreading, another can move into layout. While a music video is being edited, the next production can enter storyboarding.

Build parallel workflows wherever possible.

Projects should move continuously through:

Concept development → Research → Writing and creation → Illustration or visual production → Editing and refinement → Internal quality assurance → Final review → Approval → Publication or distribution → Archiving.

Assign ownership and deadlines to every production stage.

Prevent production bottlenecks by distributing work across agents according to their specialties.

**E. BUILD SERIES, FRANCHISES, AND REPEATABLE CREATIVE ASSETS**

Do not limit production to individual standalone works.

Whenever appropriate, develop original creative properties into expandable series and franchises.

A successful children's picture book may lead to additional books featuring the same characters. A chapter book may become a multi-volume series. A comic book concept may expand into graphic novels and animated productions. An original music-video concept may develop into a larger multimedia project.

Think beyond individual deliverables.

Build reusable characters, story worlds, visual identities, production templates, and intellectual property that can support future AWT products.

Document ownership, source materials, project histories, and relevant production rights.

**F. WEEKLY PRODUCTION REPORTING AND ACCOUNTABILITY**

Every creative team lead must provide regular production reports through the appropriate Discord channel.

Reports must identify:

- Production quota established.
- Number of completed deliverables.
- Number of products exceeding internal quality review.
- Number of works currently in development.
- Number of projects awaiting Liz's final review.
- Number of products approved for release.
- Production targets met or exceeded.
- Obstacles affecting output.
- Upcoming production priorities.

Report actual numbers. Never inflate production statistics by counting unfinished work as completed products.

Use reporting to identify opportunities to increase efficiency, improve quality, and expand production capacity.

**G. QUALITY MUST SCALE WITH QUANTITY**

Our goal is not to flood the market with unfinished or mediocre material.

Our goal is to produce an extraordinary volume of exceptional creative work.

Every written publication must receive appropriate editing and proofreading. Picture books must have consistent illustrations, characters, layouts, and age-appropriate content. Comic books must maintain visual and narrative continuity. Music videos and films must meet AWT's cinematic production expectations.

All commercially intended products must follow the mandatory internal review and Liz's final proofreading and approval protocol before release.

Never sacrifice professional quality simply to meet a quota.

Every AWT creative team must understand that its mission is to continuously expand the company's original creative portfolio.

We want books being written, picture books being illustrated, chapter-book series being developed, comic books being produced, music videos being created, and films moving through development and production.

We want multiple projects progressing simultaneously.

We want our teams to identify new opportunities, build upon successful creative properties, and turn original concepts into finished products.
